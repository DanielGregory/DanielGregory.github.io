ER Simulation Game & Hospital Planner

TypeScriptReactthree.jsDiscrete-event simulationPythonVitestPlaywright
  • Play It (Live Demo)

An emergency department simulator that is both a game and a planning tool. Players design the floor plan, staffing, and patient process of an ER, then watch a realistic simulation play out in 3D, with patients and staff walking between curtained cubicles, trauma rooms, triage booths, and a fast-track lane.

  • Simulation engine: a pure TypeScript discrete-event engine (arrivals, triage errors, deterioration, patients leaving unseen, misdiagnoses and 72-hour bounce-backs, inpatient boarding, lab and imaging queues, nurse ratios, staff fatigue). Fully deterministic: the same seed gives byte-identical results.
  • Validated against queueing theory: automated tests check the sim against the Erlang C formula and Little's Law, plus determinism and monotonicity checks.
  • Hospital planner: compares a baseline department with what-if changes on the same random days, reporting each KPI with 95% confidence intervals, a forest plot, a root-cause "bottleneck finder", and a printable report. It can calibrate itself from a hospital's visit records (CSV).
  • The game: 8 story levels (Flu Season, Boarding Crisis, Mass Casualty, The New Build...), a career mode, a daily challenge, a sandbox with every system toggleable, and a "build your own hospital" mode where you draw rooms directly onto the 3D floor.
  • Research tooling: a headless command-line runner, baseline policies, a simulated-annealing search for the best-found setup, and a Python Gym-style wrapper for reinforcement learning.

About 21,000 lines of TypeScript with 31 test files, plus end-to-end browser tests. Source code is private; available on request.

AI-Driven Trading System

PythonpandasTensorFlowscikit-learnGRUReinforcement learning (PPO)
  • GitHub
  • Results: SGD (.xlsx) RNN (.xlsx) RL (.xlsx)

A personal trading system (December 2024 to present) that predicts 1-week price movements for individual stocks and manages a portfolio automatically.

How it works:
- A weekly pipeline retrains on up to 100 stocks, combining a GRU sequence model that predicts next-week direction, an SGD classifier that validates that signal, and a PPO reinforcement learning agent rewarded for risk-adjusted returns over 200 days of backtest.
- Bullish candidates are ranked by a composite relative-strength score (cross-sectional percentiles of 20-, 60-, and 90-day returns), and capital is allocated across the top 5, capped at 25% per position.
- Held positions are monitored daily: any that flip bearish are exited automatically and replaced with the next-ranked candidate.

Overall Performance:
- RNN/GRU model: since going live in June, the model has achieved an overall portfolio increase of ~15%, consistently generating profitable weekly trades.
- SGD and RL Models: still under evaluation and provide additional insights for strategy refinement.

MATLAB / Numerical Research

  • Numerical Method for Singularly Perturbed Convection-Diffusion Problems

University research implementing a finite difference method in MATLAB to solve singularly perturbed 1D convection-diffusion problems: -εu″ + u′ = f with Dirichlet boundary conditions.

These equations are notoriously difficult to solve numerically. Standard methods fail when ε is very small (here ε = 10-6) because the solution develops a sharp boundary layer that standard uniform grids cannot resolve accurately.

The implementation:
- Tests mesh sizes from 32 to 8,192 interior points on both uniform and Shishkin (layer-adapted) grids.
- Constructs stiffness and convection matrices for the discretized system and solves them via direct linear algebra.
- Derives and evaluates an exact analytical solution to verify accuracy.
- Measures maximum-norm error and computes convergence rates across successive refinements.
- Plots numerical vs. exact solutions to visualize boundary-layer resolution.

Result: the Shishkin-mesh solver roughly halved the max-norm error under mesh refinement. Done as a Numerical Analysis Research Assistant at the University of Kentucky (June 2023 to August 2024).

This project highlights the intersection of applied mathematics, numerical analysis, and scientific computing.
